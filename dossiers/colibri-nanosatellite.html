Colibri Mission: Mexico's First 3-Unit Nanosatellite

Leading a 50-person interdisciplinary engineering team across two university campuses to design Mexico's inaugural 3U CubeSat, passing rigorous Preliminary and Critical Design Reviews evaluated by veteran aerospace experts from NASA, MIT, and the Mexican Space Agency (AEM).

DOMAIN Space Systems Engineering
TEAM SIZE 50 Engineers across 2 Campuses
REVIEW PANEL NASA, MIT & Mexican Space Agency
TIMELINE June 2018 – July 2022

01 / Background & The National Challenge

When the Colibri Mission was conceived at Universidad Panamericana, Mexico lacked an established institutional framework for universities to design, build, and fly complex multi-unit satellites. While single-unit (1U) educational satellites had been attempted, a 3-unit (3U) nanosatellite demanded rigorous space-grade systems engineering across power generation, thermal control, attitude determination and control (ADCS), telecommunications, and payload integration.

As Lead Systems Engineer, my mandate was two-fold: architect the complete technical baseline for the spacecraft, and build from scratch an organizational structure capable of coordinating 50 undergraduate and graduate engineers across two campuses (Mexico City and Aguascalientes).

02 / Systems Architecture & Technical Coordination

To ensure system coherence across diverse sub-teams, we structured the project into 10 synchronized technical disciplines:

  • Structures & Mechanisms: 3U deployable chassis, solar panel release mechanisms, and vibrational modal analysis.
  • Attitude Determination and Control (ADCS): Magnetorquers, reaction wheels, and sun sensors for precision pointing.
  • Payload Development: Miniaturized high-fidelity accelerometer and scientific imaging system for orbital testing.
  • Electrical Power System (EPS): Solar panel MPPT regulation, lithium-ion battery storage, and load shedding safety states.
  • Telemetry, Tracking & Command (TT&C): UHF/VHF and S-band communications with the ground station network.

I instituted rigorous interface control documents (ICDs), mass/power budgets, and verification matrices that maintained system integrity across hundreds of component revisions.

“Assembled and led a 50-person interdisciplinary team across two campuses to design Mexico’s first 3-unit nanosatellite, organizing 10 technical disciplines to pass design reviews against a panel of experts from MIT, NASA, and the Mexican Space Agency.” — Systems Engineering Leadership Dossier

03 / Independent Review Board: NASA, MIT & AEM

To validate the mission to flight readiness standards, we submitted our engineering baselines to an independent panel composed of senior engineers and researchers from MIT, NASA, and the Mexican Space Agency (AEM).

Our team successfully passed the formal Preliminary Design Review (PDR) and Critical Design Review (CDR), demonstrating compliant launch environment survival (random vibration, acoustic loads, thermal vacuum cycling) and fault-tolerant software architecture.

04 / Executive Advocacy & National Impact

Beyond technical engineering, systems leadership required securing the resources and partnerships necessary to sustain a multi-year aerospace program:

  • Executive Negotiations: Represented the technical team in institutional fundraising campaigns and executive-level negotiations with industrial sponsors and academic benefactors.
  • National Media Advocacy: Delivered television, radio, and print briefings articulating the strategic importance of national aerospace capacity building.
  • Scientific Publications: Presented findings at the International Astronautical Congress (IAC) IAF Materials and Structures and Space Systems Symposiums.
