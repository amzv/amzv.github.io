Lunar Surface Mobility Vehicle Fleet Architecting

An AI agent-driven vehicle family design framework leveraging Model-Based Systems Engineering (SysML v2) to optimize commonality, eliminate redundant engineering cycles, and maximize shared technology investments across Artemis-era lunar surface vehicles.

DOMAIN Space Systems & Fleet MBSE
TECHNOLOGY ENABLERS SysML v2, AI Agents, Mission Sim
DISSERTATION LEAD Angel Mario Zarate Villazon
TIMELINE November 2024 – Present

01 / Background & The Fleet Proliferation Problem

As humanity enters a permanent, multi-stakeholder era of lunar exploration through the NASA Artemis program and international commercial initiatives, lunar surface mobility requirements are expanding exponentially. Operating surface architectures require a spectrum of distinct vehicle classes: uncrewed science scouts, autonomous cargo carriers, regolith excavation rovers, and human-rated pressurized exploration platforms.

Historically, aerospace exploration has developed individual mission vehicles as bespoke, isolated programs. Developing each lunar rover in programmatic isolation, however, yields immense duplicate engineering development costs, fragmented supply chains, incompatible power/drivetrain modules, and prohibitive launch logistics.

By contrast, commercial automotive manufacturers and commercial aircraft OEMs (such as Airbus and Boeing) have long utilized product family architecting—maximizing shared underlying platforms and modular components while tailoring specialized variants to distinct market missions. My doctoral dissertation bridges this automotive and aerospace capability into lunar surface exploration.

02 / AI Agent-Driven Vehicle Family Workflow

Evaluating a multi-vehicle trade space across varying lunar mission environments (polar craters, extreme thermal cycling, prolonged darkness, high payload vs high terrain mobility) creates an enormous combinatorial optimization problem. Traditional manual trade studies cannot evaluate even a fraction of the possible modular configurations.

To solve this, I architected an AI agent-driven vehicle family design workflow that integrates:

  • Automated Stakeholder Requirement Decomposition: Translating heterogeneous mission requirements (payload mass, range, autonomy, survivability) into formal feature models.
  • SysML v2 Architecture Generation: Programmatically instantiating modular system and subsystem models in the new SysML v2 standard, guaranteeing consistency and continuous digital traceability.
  • Agentic Exploration & Multi-Objective Optimization: Autonomous agents perturb chassis geometry, wheel-soil interaction parameters, power storage chemistries, and common avionics architectures to find Pareto-optimal fleets.
  • Physics-Based Mission Simulation: Verifying energy expenditure, thermal dissipation, and slope climbing capacity across simulated lunar digital elevation maps (DEMs).
“Recognizing that family-based vehicle design processes from automotive and aerospace could eliminate duplicate work across the emerging family of lunar rover programs enables Artemis stakeholders to maximize commonality without compromising mission-specific performance.” — Dissertation Framework, Georgia Tech ASDL

03 / Core Scientific & Engineering Contributions

1. Mission-to-Feature Mapping: Established a formal mathematical methodology for extracting feature models directly from mission analysis, published at the International Council of the Aeronautical Sciences (ICAS 2026).

2. SysML v2 Integration: Early adoption and implementation of the next-generation Systems Modeling Language (SysML v2), demonstrating its power for family-based variability modeling and automated API-driven simulation execution.

3. Quantified Commonality vs Performance Pareto Fronts: Providing decision-makers with quantified trade-offs: demonstrating how accepting a 3–5% performance margin on non-critical vehicle variants can reduce fleet development costs by upwards of 35% through modular subsystem reuse.
