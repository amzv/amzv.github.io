High-Fidelity Vehicle Dynamics & Real-Time Driver-in-the-Loop Simulators

Pioneering multi-fidelity simulation architectures, optimal control lap-time optimization, and machine learning surrogate models for trackside decision support and real-time simulators with Toyota Racing Development, Honda R&D, and a NASCAR OEM.

01 / The Real-Time Simulation Dilemma

In elite motorsport and advanced automotive engineering, the quest for competitive advantage is increasingly waged inside simulation environments. High-fidelity multi-body chassis models and non-linear tire formulations (e.g., thermal-mechanical tire models with transient relaxation length and camber thrust) provide exceptional realism. However, their computational overhead historically prevented them from executing in hard real-time (1000 Hz) required for interactive Driver-in-the-Loop (DIL) simulators or high-iteration trackside setup optimizations.

Conversely, simplified point-mass or bicycle models run with instantaneous speed but fail to capture subtle transient vehicle behavior—such as curb strikes, aero pitch-sensitivity under braking, or tire grip degradation over stints—leading to sub-optimal engineering setups and poor driver correlation.

02 / Toyota Racing Development (TRD): Minimum Lap-Time Optimization

As a Vehicle Simulation Intern at Toyota Racing Development (Salisbury, NC), I worked under the mentorship of a former Formula 1 technical director to investigate novel optimization methodologies for minimum lap-time simulation (MLTS).

Using optimal control formulations and collocated transcription techniques, we examined how high-fidelity vehicle dynamic constraints could be incorporated directly into lap-time solvers without causing convergence divergence or excessive runtimes. The findings directly informed the team’s methodology for simulation-driven chassis and aero setup configurations.

“Investigated novel optimization approaches for minimum lap-time simulation using high-fidelity vehicle models under the mentorship of a former Formula 1 technical director; findings informed the team’s approach to simulation-driven setup decisions.” — Toyota Racing Development Project Log

03 / Honda R&D: Multi-Fidelity DIL Architecture

Collaborating with Honda engineers, our research team at Georgia Tech’s Aerospace Systems Design Laboratory investigated workflow inefficiencies in real-time driver-in-the-loop simulation. We conceptualized a testing workflow to identify the primary drivers of simulation error and computational cost.

To solve the fidelity-speed dilemma, we developed a tool that employed machine learning algorithms to dynamically guide modeling strategy—seamlessly substituting fast surrogates for computationally expensive sub-models when high fidelity was unneeded, and engaging high-order physics when chassis dynamics entered critical regimes. This work was published at the SAE World Congress 2026.

NASCAR OEM High-Fidelity Simulation & Tire Sensitivity

I led a multi-disciplinary graduate research team to integrate high-fidelity vehicle models into racing car simulations for a major NASCAR OEM. By combining global sensitivity analysis with machine learning surrogates, our team preserved decision-making speed without sacrificing accuracy, earning the prestigious Daniel P. Schrage Award for Excellence.

The following year, I returned as technical advisor for a repeat award-winning team, focusing specifically on high-fidelity tire models: formulating an optimal control sensitivity framework to quantify lap-time performance variance against tire model parameters, published at the American Modelica Conference and SAE Technical Paper 2026-01-0214.
