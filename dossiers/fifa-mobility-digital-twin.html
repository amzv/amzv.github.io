Mobility Digital Twin for FIFA World Cup 2026

A real-time data-calibrated decision-support framework reconciling competing priorities across stadium operations, public safety officers, and municipal transportation authorities for downtown Atlanta's mega-event operations.

DOMAIN Urban Mobility Digital Twin
STAKEHOLDERS GWCCA, GTPD, GDOT & Stadium
KEY IMPACT Days to Minutes Decision Turnaround
TIMELINE April 2025 – July 2026

01 / The Operational Challenge

With Atlanta welcoming the world for eight matches during the 2026 FIFA World Cup—the city’s largest multi-week sporting event since the 1996 Olympic Games—downtown transportation infrastructure faces an extraordinary stress test. Tens of thousands of international fans, vehicular traffic, public transit, and emergency response assets converge on the Georgia World Congress Center Authority (GWCCA) campus and Mercedes-Benz Stadium simultaneously.

Historically, event safety and traffic plans relied on static playbook assumptions constructed weeks in advance. However, mega-events inherently present fluid, non-stationary dynamics: an unexpected gate delay, road closure, weather disruption, or sudden influx of rideshare vehicles cascades throughout downtown Atlanta in minutes. Conventional traffic analysis tools required multiple days of post-hoc simulation, rendering them useless for active, on-the-ground operational decisions.

02 / Stakeholder Alignment & Decision Framing

Before writing code or architecting simulation pipelines, the foundational requirement was framing the true decision bottleneck. Reconciling competing operational priorities was essential:

  • On-the-ground Police Officers & Public Safety: Prioritized rapid crowd dispersion, clear emergency access corridors, and safety buffer zones.
  • Parking Operations & Stadium Management: Required continuous ingress throughput to avoid perimeter roadway gridlock and maximize gate utilization.
  • Georgia Department of Transportation (GDOT): Focused on arterial highway off-ramps and balancing regional commute traffic with event congestion.

Through repeated stadium site visits, shadowing officers during major events, and conducting prototype-driven interviews, we mapped the trade-offs into an interactive decision-support ontology. Rather than providing black-box outputs, the tool had to explain why a specific intervention (such as reversing lane flow on Northside Drive or gating a specific pedestrian crosswalk) would mitigate congestion without triggering secondary bottlenecks.

“Our custom-built interface allows us to build different scenarios and simulate different approaches, in real time, as conditions change. Once we determine their rules of behavior, our simulations evaluate different traffic interventions to avoid bottlenecks, traffic slowdowns, and unwanted crowdedness.” — Dr. Michael Balchanos, ASDL Senior Research Engineer & Project Lead

03 / Technical Architecture & Digital Twin Engine

The digital twin integrates multi-source empirical data feeds with high-speed behavioral simulation models:

Data Calibration: Leveraged historical and live sensor feeds, camera feeds, campus cycle track data, and event attendance profiles to calibrate agent behavioral parameters (pedestrian walking speeds, gap acceptance, vehicular following dynamics).

Real-Time Simulation Layer: Running in the background, fast-time simulation surrogate models evaluate proposed traffic interventions and predict bottleneck propagation 30 to 60 minutes into the future.

Interactive Decision Dashboard: Provided command-center operators with an intuitive interface to test "what-if" scenarios on the fly, reducing evaluation turnaround from days of manual modeling to minutes of interactive experimentation.

04 / Impact & Institutional Recognition

The mobility digital twin was delivered to the Georgia World Congress Center Authority, Mercedes-Benz Stadium operations, and the Georgia Tech Police Department. The system directly informs traffic plans and crowd management policies across all eight World Cup games.

Furthermore, as noted by university leadership, the platform provides long-term value beyond the tournament, serving as an enduring decision engine for campus growth, pedestrian infrastructure planning, and regional transit optimization.
